    01 — Overview

    Introduction

    Understanding housing trends is essential for buyers and investors. This project analyzes the housing market in Metro Manila using a dataset sourced from the Lamudi platform — employing Power BI for visualization and Python for data cleaning — to reveal key insights into property listings and market trends.

    Disclaimer: The data presented here is based on a scraped Lamudi dataset and may not fully reflect actual market conditions in Metro Manila.

    03 — Preprocessing

    Data Preprocessing

    1. Extracting City from Location

    The Location column contained full addresses. I extracted the city name by splitting on the delimiter comma and taking the last segment.

    Extracting city name from the Location column using delimiter split

    2. Filtering to Metro Manila

    I prepared a mapping of Metro Manila cities from Wikipedia, then used a left join — with the NCR cities mapping as the left table — to subset only Metro Manila listings.

    NCR cities mapping table

    Left join to filter Metro Manila listings

    3. Classifying Housing Type

    Used Text.Contains on the Description column to tag each listing as Single Attached/Detached, Townhouse, Multiplex, or Other.

    Conditional classification of housing type from description keywords

    4. Classifying Storey Level

    Applied the same Text.Contains approach to extract storey level (1-storey, 2-storey, etc.) from the description field.

    Storey level extracted from description keywords

    5. Handling Missing Prices

    About 3% of the Price column was missing. I used median imputation grouped by city — a Python script calculated each city's median price and filled nulls accordingly. 

    import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv("MM_housing_dataset.csv", encoding='ISO-8859-1')

# Step 1: Append a column to the table with the median price by city
df['price_medians_city'] = df.groupby('City')['Price'].transform('median')

# This is a variable containing the median price by city
price_medians_city = df.groupby('City')['Price'].transform('median')

# Step 2: Fill null values in the Price column with the median value by city
df['Price'] = df['Price'].fillna(price_medians_city)

# Step 3: Export the updated DataFrame to a CSV file
df.to_csv('MM_housing_dataset_imputed_2.csv', index=False)

    Python script — median imputation grouped by city

    05 — Analysis & Findings

    Key Findings

    Median Prices by City

    The overall median house price in Metro Manila was ₱28.65 million, with a median of ₱212,580 per sqm. Makati City stood out with a staggering median of ₱152M and ₱643,600/sqm — driven by its central business district and luxury villages. Taguig (Bonifacio Global City) followed at ₱112M median and ₱280,500/sqm.

    Most affordable cities: Marikina (₱9.7M), Valenzuela (₱7.4M), and Caloocan (₱4.5M) — making them attractive for buyers on a budget.

    Median price by city

    Median price per sqm by city

    Total Listings by City

    Muntinlupa led with 140 listings, followed by Quezon City (117) and Parañaque (68). These suburban cities offer larger land areas and more active residential developments compared to Makati or Taguig, where space is constrained and prices are higher.

    Total listings by city

    06 — Insights

    What the Data Suggests

    Price polarization is stark. Makati and Taguig operate in an entirely different tier — their medians are 4–5× the Metro Manila average. These aren't outliers; they reflect fundamentally different buyer segments.
    Listings ≠ demand. The cities with the most listings (Muntinlupa, QC, Parañaque) are not the most expensive. High listing volume in suburban areas signals supply-driven availability, not premium pricing.

    For buyers seeking value, the data points clearly to Marikina, Valenzuela, and Caloocan as the most affordable options in the metro. For investors targeting high-value assets, Makati and Taguig remain the premium markets — but entry prices reflect that.
